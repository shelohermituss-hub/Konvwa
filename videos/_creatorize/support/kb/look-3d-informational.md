# 3D Informational (Zack D Like)
keywords: 3d informational, zack d, zack d films, informational, megaproject, engineering, what if, explainer, realistic, realistic 3d, blender, eevee, x-ray, green highlight, camera, mount everest, install blender, slow export, quality, standard, verified, ultraedit
platforms: both

**3D Informational (Zack D Like)**: realistic 3D explainers rendered in Blender. A big "what if" idea (moving a
mountain, building a roof against the weather) is built step by step on screen: machines, rock, snow, steel, water,
a globe. The part the voice names glows in see-through green on its word, and a calm camera glides through every
shot. Pick it in **New project**. It makes **Vertical 9:16** or **Wide 16:9** (the
**Format** switch).

## Quality

Picked in New project, changeable in the **Build** dialog:

- **Standard**: one Claude session builds and checks every shot, with detailed target pictures for the most
  important shots. The fastest and lightest on your Claude plan.
- **Verified**: a detailed target picture for every shot, built for real, and every shot checked at its start,
  middle and end.
- **Ultraedit**: Verified plus a second full round on the weakest shots. The best result, the most Claude usage.

The target pictures use your image service (Google Flow is free with your own account: Settings > Media > Image
generation). Without one, Claude builds the detail from its own judgement.

## Preview

Blender runs hidden: under the preview, **Solid** is a fast light shading for scrubbing, **Look** shows the real
light when the playhead rests. Click a machine or object to select it for Claude.

## Installing Blender

Creatorize installs Blender itself the first time you need it (the official build from blender.org, about 400 MB,
once): **Install Blender** in New project, or by itself when you create the project. **Mac**: Blender 5.2 needs a Mac
with Apple silicon (M1 or newer).

## Exporting takes longer

Blender renders every frame for real: on a fast graphics card a 1-minute video takes about 5-10 minutes, on a laptop
several times longer. Frames already rendered are kept, so exporting again after a small change is quicker.
