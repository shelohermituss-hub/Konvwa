## Write it with Claude

1. In the Pipeline click **Script written > Write**.
2. Fill in **Topic**, **Length** (minutes:seconds, e.g. 2:30) and, if you like, **Angle and notes**.
3. Click **Write script**. Claude researches the topic on the web and writes the narration at that length.

## Read, edit or rewrite

**Script written > Open** shows the **Script** window:
- **Narration**: edit the text and click **Save**. The top shows the spoken words and about how long they take (about 150 words a minute in English).
- **Research & sources**: Claude's notes and sources.
- **Rewrite with Claude**: add new notes and click **Rewrite script**; Claude revises the script (your unsaved edits are saved first).
- **Copy for TTS**: copies only the spoken words (headings, [notes], (directions), markdown marks and emoji are left out), for a voice tool elsewhere.
- **Make the voiceover**: saves and opens the Voiceover window.
- **Open file**: opens the script in your text app.

## Your own script

In **New project**, under **Story**, pick **I have a script** and paste the exact narration. Once a project has a script, you can also replace its text in **Narration**.

## Your own scripting style

**Settings > AI engines > Your scripting skill**: **Add a file (.md or .txt)**, **Add a skill folder** or **Paste text** (how you open, the tone, the structure, words to avoid). Claude follows it in every script it writes or rewrites; the length and language still apply. For one video without it, untick **Use my scripting skill … for this video** in the write window.

## Changing the script after the voice

The voiceover still says the old words. Make the voiceover again (with ElevenLabs and Fish Audio, unchanged parts are reused for free); keep **Then clean the audio and time the words** ticked so the scenes follow the new words.
