# Investigation look: the investigative script

For projects in the Investigation look (`settings.theme === 'investigation'`). Measured on a 23-minute reference
documentary (3,633 words, 354+ shots): 81% of the words are the narrator, ~175 words per minute inside a paragraph, a
median sentence of 12 words, a hard fact every 8.5 s, a name or number in half the sentences. The structure below is that
film's, generalised; the rules are what made it work. The storyboard step plans its shots from the tags this script carries
(references/investigation_shots.md), so the tags are part of the script, not decoration.

**Any topic.** The reference was a crime documentary, but the Investigation look is a visual style, not a genre: the arc
below works for any story (a team's championship season, a company's rise and fall, a scientific race, a historic
disaster, a crime). Write each beat in the story's own terms. Crime words (perpetrator, suspects, the crime, the raid, the
arrest, the charges) only when the story IS about a crime or an investigation; never invent one. The examples in
brackets show the crime version and another topic's version side by side.

## The shape
Opens on the climax, not at the beginning. Two braided protagonists (or a protagonist and the people who found out: rivals,
reporters, investigators), one withheld identity or fact, long open loops planted early and paid late, a foreshadow line
before a break, hubris or a turn, the outcome, a short coda aimed at the viewer. Time order jumps (e.g. 2015 -> 2008 -> 2013 -> 2015 -> 2016 -> 2023): every jump starts with a date and a place.
The climax starts at ~63% of the runtime, the outcome at ~83%, the coda is the last ~5%.

## The 14 beats and their word budgets
Narration words (not headings, tags or [notes]). The 12-minute master is ~1,770 words: ~175 wpm inside paragraphs and
~148 wpm over the whole track once the title, breaks and quotes are in. 10 minutes = x0.83, 15 minutes = x1.25; any other
length: scale every beat by (target words / 1,770).

**Short videos (under ~4 minutes)** compress the arc to six beats and a break, same rules: cold open with date + stakes
(15%), setup (15%), the central event (the crime; the game) (20%), [BREAK 2s], the turn and the clue (the investigation;
the comeback) (20%), the reveal of the withheld identity or fact as the last words of a sentence (15%), aftermath + coda (15%). The 60-second Mona Lisa test is this shape
(experiments/crime-style/test60).

## The 32 writing rules
1. Open with "It's [Month Year], [sensory detail]." Never a question, never a channel intro.
2. Stakes and the institution by second 30, with the "If X hadn't been X... But X happens to be..." turn.
3. Withhold one identity for 45-90 s; reveal it as the last words of a sentence.
4. The cold open lists three things; each one is paid off later.
5. Before the midpoint, plant 2-3 objects or habits that decide the ending; at the payoff, repeat the same words.
6. At ~45% put a foreshadow line ("Ultimately, it won't be enough.") straight before a break, with a flash-forward.
7. Historical present tense; the past tense only for the aftermath.
8. Sentence length: median 12 words, ~15% at 6 or fewer, ~10% at 21 or more, none over 35.
9. Long-long-short: every 3-6 sentence micro-beat ends on a 2-5 word punch ("It works." "He blacks out.").
10. At most one rhetorical question per 3 minutes.
11. Every chapter begins with a date and a place.
12. First mention: "[role], [name]" ("the head of the CIA, John Brennan"); the surname only after that.
13. A character back after 2+ minutes gets their role epithet again.
14. Only 3-4 characters carry the story; everyone else is a role ("an FBI agent").
15. Concrete numbers; money also in today's value; no more than 2 numbers per sentence.
16. The big number goes last in its sentence.
17. A hard fact about every 8 s of narration; about half the sentences carry a name or a number.
18. Technology in two steps: "X is essentially..." then "You could use it to...".
19. "You" for explanations; "we" only for the channel's own checking ("We asked the FBI. They did not answer.").
20. Name the sources aloud once or twice in the video (a court filing, a newspaper, an interview).
21. Dry humour about every 2 minutes, aimed at power, never at victims.
22. Lists of three.
23. One aphorism per act.
24. The outcome's key moments (a raid, an arrest; the last play, the vote): 4-8 word sentences, present tense.
25. A real, sourced quote in the subject's own words every 2-3 minutes, on its own line as [QUOTE: name] before it.
26. [BREAK 4s] after each cliffhanger line, [BREAK 8-12s] after the climax and after the outcome (about one per 90 s, more in the outcome beat).
27. Every sentence gets one visual tag (below), in square brackets at its start.
28. Long open loops (10-20 min) are planted in the first third and paid in the last; short loops (under 90 s) withhold a name.
29. The climax calls back the cold open: the same words, so the same pictures can return.
30. The coda: the moral + a dated fact with a number + one line aimed at the viewer. No summary, no "subscribe".
31. No gore, no abuse described in detail: a headline carries it. Victims or losers are named with care, never joked about.
32. No hype words ("shocking", "insane", "crazy"), no "little did they know", no "in this video".

## Visual tags (one per sentence, in brackets, not read aloud)
`[MANNEQUIN]` a reenactment with the porcelain figures · `[PHOTO name]` a real photo of a named person, on the name ·
`[DOC]` a document on the desk with the key phrase highlighted · `[HEADLINE]` a press headline card · `[UI]` a screen /
login / search / post · `[GRID]` the red-grid network (who connects to whom, money or data flow) · `[BOARD]` the pinned
board (the story's players, events and clues on string; suspects only in a crime story) · `[PLACE+DATE]` an establishing shot with the place/date tag · `[STOCK]` real b-roll
of a general idea · `[ARCHIVE]` real footage of the event · `[SIDEVIEW]` a side cutaway of a building · `[MAP]` a real map
· `[QUOTE: name]` a real quote card · `[CHAPTER: title]` a chapter card (in a break) · `[LIST]` one picture per item.
Example:
```
## The empty wall
[PLACE+DATE] It's Monday, August 21st, 1911.
[MANNEQUIN] A man in a white smock lifts a small painting off four iron pegs.
[PHOTO Mona Lisa] She's the Mona Lisa.
[BREAK 2s]
```
Brackets are never read by the voice. `[BREAK Ns]` marks a music-only pause; the storyboard puts the chapter card, the
black hold or the flash-forward there, and the build checks audio/timeline.json for the real gap before it places black.

## Pauses (for the voice and Trim silences)
- Between sentences ~0.30 s (vary 0.26-0.36); after a comma 0.10-0.25 s (never under 0.08); paragraph ends ~0.8 s.
- A mid-sentence gap over 0.35 s that is not at punctuation is a TTS glitch: close it to ~0.05 s.
- Keep 40-60 ms before a word starts and 80 ms after the last word; breaks are music, never trimmed.
- Targets: ~175 wpm inside paragraphs, 145-155 wpm over the whole track with the breaks.
