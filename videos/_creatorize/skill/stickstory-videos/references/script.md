# The script: "What if you..."

Measured on the three references (experiments/stickstory-style/analysis/story NOTES.md §1, BUILD IMPLICATIONS). A builder
reads this to understand the story it pictures; a script writer follows it. The two example scripts:
experiments/stickstory-style/videos/01-pause/script.txt and 02-handshake/script.txt.

## 1. The premise

"What if you ..." + ONE extreme, quantified condition (an IQ number, sixty seconds a day, a hundred lives) applied to an
ORDINARY life (a kitchen, a school, a job). The video is an essay on what the condition would really feel like, which
turns into one personal story where the condition is finally used for someone else. The spine: the power isolates you ->
a loved one is hurt by something you could not foresee -> you use the power selflessly -> the spark you were missing
returns. Then a twist or a sequel hook, and one question to the viewer.

## 2. The file format (script.txt, as the app voices it)

```
Every raindrop in the city just stopped.
A man, halfway through a sneeze.
Everyone, except you.
(beat 1.5s)

In one minute, your heart beats about seventy times.
[Poppy] Kit! Do the magic!
[Mum] Not at breakfast.
[You] Close your eyes, Pops.
(beat 2s)
```

- One sentence per line. A blank line between the 12 story beats (§4).
- `[Name] line` = that character speaks in its own voice (Voiceover > Character voices); the tag is never spoken or
  counted. `[You]` is the hero's own voice (only in dialogue; the narrator says "you").
- `(beat Ns)` = N seconds of real silence (kept by the app's clean step): a word-free beat the pictures fill with action
  or a reaction. 31-33 short beats of 2-4 s (one every ~12 s of speech) + 2-3 long ones of 8-14 s at the climaxes + a 3 s
  tail on the end card.
- Numbers are WRITTEN AS WORDS ("seventy", "eighteen hundred kilometres") so the voice reads them right; the pictures show
  them as digits.

## 3. Length and sentences

| measure | target |
|---|---|
| words for 8:00 | **850 (+-50)**: ~600 narrator + ~250 dialogue |
| sentences | ~155, ~45 of them dialogue; 4-6 character voices + the narrator |
| sentence length | mean 5.3 words; 30 % are 1-3 words; <= 5 % over 12; hard cap 17 |
| questions | 10-15, most rhetorical and answered at once ("The catch?") |
| numbers | 2-4 a minute, concrete and oddly specific (60 degrees, 31 moves, 40 phones, 300 names) |
| pace | the voice fast inside a sentence (185-200 wpm), ~0.75 s between sentences, ~100 wpm over the whole video |

- Second person, PRESENT tense for the hero ("You close your fist. Everything stops."). Past tense only for backstory.
  One deliberate point-of-view break is allowed (a first-person cold open, a third-person final twist).
- The hero has a short name only OTHER characters say; the narrator says "you".
- Side characters: 4-7, each introduced in a tiny exchange of 2-4 lines within the first minute, each with one trait and
  one prop (the mother with her glasses and two jobs, the little sister with her hair clip). Names of 1-2 syllables, our
  own.
- The narrator is calm, low and close; deadpan on the jokes; slower and quieter on the emotional beats. Energy comes from
  the editing, not the voice.

## 4. The 12-beat sheet (8:00)

| # | beat | time | words | pictures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HOOK: 6-8 concrete fragments showing the condition in daily life (or a flash-forward to the climax); a deadpan punchline | 0:00-0:20 | 40 | one insert or reaction per fragment, ~1.5 s shots |
| 2 | FACT FRAME + PREMISE: real-world numbers, then the extreme number; the title "What if you ..." | 0:20-0:35 | 25 | a full-frame chart / map, the title over a wide, a boom on the reveal |
| 3 | RULES + WORLD: how it works; the daily life; 2 tiny dialogue exchanges (plant 1) | 0:35-1:15 | 85 | home set, speaker close-ups, the HUD counter appears |
| 4 | PLAY: "Plus," a montage of fun consequences, the counter ticking (plant 2) | 1:15-2:20 | 140 | 15-25 short shots, the + icon, prediction tags |
| 5 | COST: "Minus," / "The catch?"; one science fact about why it hurts; an analogy | 2:20-3:20 | 120 | the - icon, grey / memory moods, a diagram |
| 6 | HEART: the loved one's small sacrifices; a setup for tonight (plant 3) | 3:20-3:55 | 60 | warm interiors, hand inserts with keepsakes |
| 7 | INCITING HIT on the loved one, something the power did not foresee; mostly dialogue | 3:55-4:35 | 80 | phone UI, a split screen, close-ups; the music drops |
| 8 | LOW POINT + MORAL CHOICE: the easy cruel option vs the right one | 4:35-5:05 | 50 | a dark set, 1-2 long silences, the two option pills |
| 9 | PLAN / ACTION: the power used outward for the first time | 5:05-6:15 | 110 | maps, diagrams, a timer, fast inserts, 2-3 silent action runs, scope bars |
| 10 | PAYOFF + TWIST: the villain undone, the scale shown | 6:15-6:55 | 60 | a news chyron, a counter montage, a big boom |
| 11 | RESOLUTION: the reunion; the missing spark returns; 2 callbacks paid | 6:55-7:30 | 70 | a warm wide two / three-shot, a close-up smile |
| 12 | SEQUEL HOOK / FINAL TWIST + the question + comments + like / subscribe | 7:30-8:00 | 40 | silent text or a reset, then the end card (8-11 s) |

Shape in % of the runtime: hook <= 6 | rules by 15 | play 15-30 | cost 30-50 | the loved one by 45 | the hit 50-60 | low
point + choice 60-67 | action 65-80 | payoff 77-87 | twist 90-97 | the question in the last 2-3 %.

## 5. The devices

One counter that runs the whole video; prediction tags with percentages; corner time / place labels; Plus / Minus / The
catch? / The price? sections; one-word rhetorical questions answered at once; at least 3 plants paid off (the strongest
in the last 20 %); one news chyron; phone UI for every message; one real fact per section and one public-service line;
one side character with a repeated catchphrase.

## 6. The ending

The emotional resolution -> a twist or sequel hook -> ONE direct question to the viewer tied to the theme ("What would you
do with sixty seconds?") -> comments -> like / subscribe (+ "part two" when a sequel is planned): 12-21 words, starting
8-11 s before the end, spoken over the end card.

## 7. Never

Self-harm (keep the stakes to loss, danger, illness, injustice); real people, brands or channels; AI-voice notices.
