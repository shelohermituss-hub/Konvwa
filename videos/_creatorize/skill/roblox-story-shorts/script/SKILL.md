# Roblox story script

You write the narration for a Roblox story short: blocky R6 avatars with 2D decal faces act out every sentence
literally, cut every 1-2 s, white captions in the upper third. The script IS the storyboard: if a clause can't be
filmed, it can't be in the script. Read `references/formula.md` (the numbers) and skim `references/examples.md`
(the 9 references, beat by beat) before writing.

## The 5 core rules
1. **Length and pace.** Narration runs at **258 wpm** (measured 244-271). Short = **150-220 words = 35-52 s**
   (default). Long = **450-490 words = 105-115 s** (first person "I", one family adventure). Seconds = words / 4.3.
2. **Rule in sentence 1, before second 5.** "In this world, [everyday act] [literal consequence]." (13-20 words)
   or "Ever since [Name] was [age], ...". Then sized in 1-2 parallel lines ("Small lie, a few inches. Big lie, the ceiling.").
3. **Fixed 7-beat spine.** RULE (0-8 %) -> SCALE -> 3-5 escalating WORLD GAGS (to ~35 %) -> named HERO and his
   extreme relation to the rule -> TURN "Then one day..." (~50 %) -> COMPLICATION with short dialogue -> TWIST in the
   last 5-15 %.
4. **Short, filmable sentences.** Average **6-10 words**, at least **1/3 of sentences 6 words or fewer**, none over
   **25**. One clause = one shot (~6 words, 1.2-1.9 s). Every clause names someone or something doing a visible thing.
5. **End on a 2-7 word line** that either reframes the rule ("It was don't cry alone.") or applies it one more time,
   deadpan ("Dad was on sale."). Nothing after it: no moral, no question, no call to action.

## Workflow
1. Pick a rule: an everyday act (lying, crying, whistling, yawning, saying "I love you") + a literal, physical,
   filmable consequence (float, vanish, grow flowers, turn to mannequin). It must be visible on a Roblox avatar.
2. Decide the twist first (formula.md: reframe / weapon / literal button). Plant its seed as a harmless gag early.
3. List 6 world gags, keep the best 3-5, order them small -> absurd.
4. Hero: short US first name, age as a number, the extreme case of the rule (never cried, jumpiest, only plaid).
5. Write. Then cut every sentence over 15 words that isn't the hook. Read it aloud at 258 wpm.
6. Title + shot hints + checklist. Output in the format below.

## Humor (use at least 4 per script)
Literal consequence followed one step further; society calmly adapted ("It's on the news.", "mostly ladders");
understatement buttons ("She was not happy.", "Nobody has explained that one."); exact numbers instead of adjectives
(1,200 years, 70 % off, 11 more times); rule of three with a broken third; deadpan dialogue ping-pong where the calm
one wins; a callback from the gags at the end; absurd escalation lists (40 ft, 60 ft, the water tower).
Tone: deadpan narrator, never laughs at his own joke, never says "funny" or "hilarious".

## Title format
`You Can't CRY...`, `If You LIE, You FLOAT...`, `Every Secret Grows a FLOWER...`: 2-6 words, ONE caps keyword
(the act or the consequence), ends with "...". Second person or rule-shaped. No emojis, no hashtags, no "POV".

## Names and words
Names: Leo, Ryan, Tyler, Dion, Joey, Daniel, Marcus, Ms. Larkin (pick fresh ones of that kind: Max, Eli, Nate,
Jayden, Mia, Ava, Mr. Patel). Family by role (mom, dad, sister, grandma). Grade 3-5 vocabulary, contractions, past
tense. Settings the kit can build: school hallway with lockers, classroom, bedroom, living room, kitchen, mall,
outside street / parking lot, park, fair.

## Avoid
- Sentences over 25 words; abstract lines with nothing to film ("He reflected on life.").
- Explaining the joke or the twist after the last line; a moral; "And that's how..."; questions to the viewer.
- Gore, real violence on kids, real brands, real people, slurs, romance between kids, scary content aimed at small kids beyond the monster level.
- Copying a reference premise (floating liars, no crying, love = days, whistling, flowers, shirt colors, mall mannequins, jump scares, school monster).
- Settings or effects the scene builder can't make (crowds of 100, oceans, space battles). Max 6 named/visible characters.
- Em dashes, emojis, "like and subscribe", "In today's video".

## Output format (what the scene builder reads)
```
TITLE: You Can't YAWN...
MODE: narrated short | WORDS: 172 | EST: 40 s (172 / 4.3)
CAST: max (hero, 12, red hoodie, sleepy face) | mom | ms_ruiz (teacher, glasses) | ...
SETS: classroom (day), bedroom (night), ...

[RULE] In this world, if someone sees you yawn, they fall asleep on the spot.
  > SHOT: classroom, max yawns big (face: yawn) -> cut: kid behind him drops face-first on desk (face: asleep, "Zzz")
[SCALE] One yawn, one nap.
  > SHOT: close-up, one kid asleep, a "1" pops over his head
```
Rules for hints: one `> SHOT:` per clause or sentence; say the SET, WHO, ACTION, FACE (decal: smug, shocked,
crying, angry, happy, asleep, scared, deadpan), CAMERA when it matters (low angle, close-up, over-shoulder, wide),
and any TEXT on screen (numbers, sounds like "boing", labels). Keep each hint under 25 words.

## Checklist (all must be yes)
- [ ] Rule fully stated in sentence 1, before second 5
- [ ] Words in range (150-220 short / 450-490 long) and EST = words / 4.3 written
- [ ] Average sentence 6-10 words, a third 6 or fewer, none over 25
- [ ] 3-5 world gags, escalating, the last the most absurd
- [ ] Hero named, age as a number, is the extreme case of the rule
- [ ] Turn starts with "Then one ..." / "But one day" / a dated moment, around the middle
- [ ] At least 4 humor devices, at least 2 exact numbers, at least 1 short dialogue exchange
- [ ] Twist type chosen, seed planted earlier, last line 2-7 words, nothing after it
- [ ] Every clause has a SHOT hint naming set, who, action, face
- [ ] Original premise, nothing from the Avoid list, title in format

## Example 1: You Can't YAWN...
MODE: narrated short | WORDS: 164 | EST: 38 s | twist: weapon + literal button
CAST: nate (hero, 13, green hoodie) | mom | coach_reed (gym teacher, whistle) | 3 classmates | the principal
SETS: classroom, bedroom, school gym, principal's office

[RULE] In this world, if someone sees you yawn, they fall asleep right where they're standing.
  > SHOT: classroom wide, a girl yawns, the boy facing her drops face-first on his desk (face: asleep)
[SCALE] Small yawn, a nap. Big yawn, the whole room.
  > SHOT: one kid snoozing, "1" over his head -> cut: whole class face-down, "Zzz" over everyone
[GAGS] So teachers aren't allowed to yawn.
  > SHOT: teacher at board pinching her own mouth shut (face: strained)
Bus drivers wear masks.
  > SHOT: bus driver in a big surgical mask, thumbs up (face: deadpan)
Every library has a guy whose whole job is telling jokes.
  > SHOT: library, man in a suit with a microphone, students staring at books (face: bored)
And pillow stores are guarded like banks.
  > SHOT: pillow shop with a security guard and a velvet rope (face: serious)
[HERO] Nate was the sleepiest kid in his school.
  > SHOT: hallway, nate leaning on his locker, eyes half closed (face: sleepy)
He'd once knocked out a whole birthday party during the candles.
  > SHOT: party, cake with candles, nate yawns, eight kids drop onto the table
His mom made him wear a scarf over his face until he was ten.
  > SHOT: kitchen, little nate in a huge striped scarf up to his eyes, mom nodding
[TURN] Then one Friday, the gym teacher announced a fitness test.
  > SHOT: gym, coach_reed blows whistle, "FITNESS TEST" on a whiteboard
A mile run. Two hundred push-ups. No breaks.
  > SHOT: three quick cuts: track, a kid on the floor doing push-ups, the whiteboard "NO BREAKS" (face: shocked kids)
[COMPLICATION] The whole class looked at Nate.
  > SHOT: low angle, twelve kids turn their heads at once (face: begging)
Nate said, I'm not tired.
  > SHOT: close-up nate (face: deadpan)
A kid said, think about math.
  > SHOT: classmate whispering in his ear (face: hopeful)
Nate thought about math.
  > SHOT: nate staring, floating numbers around his head (face: sleepy)
[TWIST] He yawned so big his hoodie stretched.
  > SHOT: extreme close-up, mouth wide (face: yawn), hood strings flying
The coach fell asleep mid-whistle.
  > SHOT: coach_reed on the floor, whistle still in his mouth, "Zzz"
So did the principal, who was watching from the door.
  > SHOT: doorway, the principal slides down the frame, asleep
Nobody ran a mile that day.
  > SHOT: wide gym, everyone asleep on the mats except nate
Nate got an A. He was the only one awake to grade.
  > SHOT: nate at the coach's desk writing a big red "A" on his own paper (face: smug)

## Example 2: Your SHADOW Talks...
MODE: narrated short | WORDS: 156 | EST: 36 s | twist: reframe
CAST: mia (hero, 11, purple braids, yellow shirt) | her shadow (flat black copy) | dad | eli (new kid, quiet) | teacher
SETS: street (day), classroom, cafeteria, bedroom (night)

[RULE] In this world, your shadow says out loud whatever you're really thinking.
  > SHOT: street, a man smiles at his neighbor, his shadow on the wall shouts "I hate your dog"
[SCALE] In the sun, it whispers. Under a spotlight, it yells.
  > SHOT: shadow whispering in daylight -> cut: stage, spotlight, giant shadow with a megaphone
[GAGS] So job interviews happen in the dark.
  > SHOT: office with the lights off, two flashlight faces (face: nervous)
First dates are only at noon, when shadows are smallest.
  > SHOT: park bench, sun straight up, tiny shadows mumbling
And every school photo has a teacher holding a lamp in exactly the right spot.
  > SHOT: photographer, teacher crouching with a lamp aimed at kids' feet (face: focused)
[HERO] Mia's shadow had never said a mean thing.
  > SHOT: mia waving, her shadow waving and saying "nice shoes"
Not once. Teachers loved her. Her dad called her the nicest kid alive.
  > SHOT: teacher giving a gold star -> cut: dad hugging her (face: proud)
[TURN] Then one Monday, a new kid sat next to her at lunch.
  > SHOT: cafeteria, eli sits down with a tray, says nothing (face: shy)
He didn't have a shadow at all.
  > SHOT: low angle, bright window, mia has a shadow, eli has none (face: mia shocked)
[COMPLICATION] Mia said, where's your shadow?
  > SHOT: over-shoulder on eli (face: mia curious)
Eli said, it left.
  > SHOT: close-up eli (face: deadpan)
Her shadow said, that's the saddest thing I've ever heard.
  > SHOT: mia's shadow on the wall, crying shadow tears
Mia's face went red. The whole table stared.
  > SHOT: four kids turn (face: shocked), mia (face: embarrassed)
[TWIST] Eli looked at her shadow and smiled for the first time.
  > SHOT: eli (face: small smile)
Shadows don't leave mean people.
  > SHOT: dark street at night, a lone shadow walking away from a house
They leave people nobody ever talks to.
  > SHOT: eli alone at an empty table, old memory tint
The next day, Eli had a shadow again.
  > SHOT: cafeteria, sunlight, eli's shadow is back next to mia's
It wouldn't stop talking.
  > SHOT: two shadows chatting on the wall, mia and eli laughing (face: happy)

## Example 3: Late By One MINUTE... (LONG, first person)
MODE: narrated long | WORDS: ~470 (write the full version at 450-490) | EST: ~109 s | twist: weapon + literal button
Outline with sample lines (expand each beat to its share; keep every rule above):
- [RULE] "In this town, if you're late, you age one year for every minute." > SHOT: alarm clock 7:01, a kid grows a grey beard at the bus stop
- [SCALE] "Five minutes late, you look like your dad. Ten, your grandpa." > SHOT: three kids in a row, each older, same backpack
- [GAGS] "Every wall has three clocks." / "Traffic lights count down in seconds and people cry at them." / "Weddings start an hour early, just in case." > SHOT per line
- [HERO] "My grandpa is 41." "He was late to a job interview in 1997." > SHOT: grandpa with a young face and a cane (face: deadpan)
- [TURN] "Last Thursday, our power went out and every clock in the house stopped at 6:40." > SHOT: dark bedroom, clocks frozen
- [COMPLICATION] countdown lines with times (7:52, 7:56, 7:58), obstacles (a goose blocks the sidewalk, the crossing guard is very slow), "I could see the school doors, 30 feet away, then 10."
- [TWIST] "Grandpa grabbed my arm. Go late, he said. He walked through the doors at 8:03 and got three years older. He looked 44. He said he'd never felt better." last line: "He's the youngest old man in town." > SHOT: grandpa flexing in the hallway (face: smug)

## Mode 2: meme / no narration (music + captions only)
Studied from momyelling, iqbarrier, testpaper (refs/<n>/sheets): 12-16 s, no voice, a trending song does the
timing. Write it as a caption list, not a script:
- A fixed **top caption** for the whole video (the premise): "Mom yelling at the family", "When Noob received the
  test paper", or a HUD label that changes ("IQ ?" -> "IQ: 67" -> "IQ: 167" -> "Normal Person").
- A **role label** per segment under it: "Big bro:", "Dad:", "Big sis:", "Lil bro:". One segment = 2-4 s = one
  reaction, 3-5 segments, the last one breaks the pattern (everyone hides, lil bro ... gets caught).
- The joke is purely physical and each segment escalates: hide in the toilet -> flush himself away; climb into a
  fighter jet and fly off; hide under the bed; the last one fails. Faces do the acting (screaming mom, wide-eyed kids).
- Output format: `TOP: <caption>` then lines `[0.0-3.0] LABEL: Big bro: | SHOT: bathroom, big bro dives head-first into toilet, flushes (face: panic)`.
- Song: name a mood only (upbeat trend / dramatic); the member picks the track. No lyrics in captions.
