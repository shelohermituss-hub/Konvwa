## The numbers to write to
- **Pace:** 244-271 wpm, median **258 wpm** (fast TTS-style narration, no pauses over 0.35 s anywhere).
  Duration (s) = words / 4.3. Always state the estimate.
- **Two lengths:**
  - **Short** (default): **150-220 words = 35-52 s**, 15-28 sentences.
  - **Long** (first-person adventure): **450-490 words = 105-115 s**, 65-75 sentences.
- **Sentences:** average 6-10 words, median 6-8, never over 25 (the one 32-word sentence is monster's opening).
  At least a third of sentences are 6 words or fewer (half in long ones).
- **Last line:** 3-7 words, ends the video. No outro, no "like and subscribe", no moral spelled out after it.
- **Hook:** the rule is fully stated in sentence 1 (13-20 words), before second 5.

## Structure (time = share of the script)
| beat | share | what | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. RULE | 0-8 % | "In this world, ..." / "Ever since X was 7, ..." / "In this town, ..." / "If you ..., you ...". One sentence, concrete cause -> concrete effect. | "In this world, nobody is allowed to cry." |
| 2. SCALE / MECHANICS | 8-15 % | 1-2 lines that size it: "Small lie, a few inches. Big lie, the ceiling." / "Little secrets grow a bud. Big ones bloom." | flower, levitate, jumpscared |
| 3. WORLD GAGS | 15-35 % | 3-5 escalating literal consequences on society, each 1 sentence, the last the most absurd. | "So onions are illegal here. Funerals happen over the phone." |
| 4. HERO | 35-45 % | Name + the hero's relation to the rule (the extreme case: never lies, jumpiest kid, only plaid shirt) + 1-3 personal gags. | "Dion hadn't cried since he was four." |
| 5. TURN | 45-60 % | "Then one day / one Tuesday / one night / Last Saturday" + the event that breaks the routine. | "Then one Tuesday, he found out his best friend was moving" |
| 6. COMPLICATION | 60-85 % | Rule fires at full force, people react (scream, faint), dialogue in short quoted lines. | bully floats higher with each lie |
| 7. TWIST / PUNCHLINE | last 5-15 % | 1-3 lines that reframe the rule or land the joke. Final line short. | "The rule was never don't cry. It was don't cry alone." |

## The twist: three kinds (pick one before writing)
1. **Reframe the rule** (heartfelt): the rule meant something else all along. cantcry ("don't cry alone"),
   monster ("it was protecting them"), flower (the heaviest secrets are other people's), whistle (who misses you most
   is someone you forgot). Setup: plant the hidden truth early as a harmless gag (flower: brother "told not to worry").
2. **Rule used as a weapon / tool** (triumph): the hero turns the rule around. levitate (keep lying, keep rising),
   jumpscared (mom's full name = the biggest scare), shirtcolor (plaid is the strongest).
3. **Literal rule + deadpan button** (pure joke): the rule's logic applied one step further. love ("He gained a
   day too"), statue ("Dad was on sale"), shirtcolor ("They just found his shoes").
Best ones stack 1 or 2 with a final 3 (statue: rescue + "Dad was on sale").

## Humor devices (each script uses at least 4)
- **Literal consequence:** follow the rule to its practical end ("movie theater only shows comedies and there's a man at the door who checks").
- **Bureaucratic normality:** society calmly adapted ("It's on the news", "The food court only sells food you can eat while running", "mostly ladders").
- **Understatement:** "She was not happy." "Nobody has explained that one." "He was a problem."
- **Specific numbers:** 1,200 years, 70 % off, 11 more times, 40 kids, 8:55. A number beats an adjective.
- **Rule of three, third breaks:** "four husbands, nine presidents, and an entire town that no longer exists."
- **Deadpan dialogue ping-pong:** short alternating lines, the calm character wins ("I'm affectionate." / "those are the same thing").
- **Hero's deadpan line to the victim:** "You should stop talking. You're running out of ceiling."
- **Callback:** a gag from beat 3 pays off at the end (shoes, ceiling, "say my whole name").
- **Absurd escalation in a list:** 40 ft, 60 ft, water tower.

## Names
Short, common US first names, one per character: Leo, Ryan, Tyler, Dion, Joey, Daniel, Marcus, Ms. Larkin,
Mr. Daniels, Benjamin. Family by role: mom, dad, sister, grandma, little brother. Full name only for a gag
("Benjamin, Alexander, Perez"). Ages as numbers: "15-year-old Tyler", "since Leo was seven".

## Vocabulary
Grade 3-5 words. Everyday settings: school hallway, lockers, classroom, bedroom, living room, mall, gas station,
fair. Verbs you can film: floated, screamed, fainted, ran, hid, grabbed, jumped. No slang beyond "like an idiot".
Contractions always. Past tense (short form third person; long form first person "I", "my dad").

## Visualisability (the shot rule)
- One clause = one shot (scenes average 1.2-1.9 s; 255 wpm x 1.5 s = ~6 words per shot).
- Every clause must name a person, a place or a thing the camera can show doing something. Abstract lines
  ("Leo stopped hanging around people. It was easier.") still need a picture: hero alone on a bench.
- Numbers become HUD text / props (price tag, timer, height counter, "+1 day" counter).
- Sounds become text: "boing", "bong".
