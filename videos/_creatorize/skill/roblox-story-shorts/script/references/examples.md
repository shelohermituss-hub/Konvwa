# The 9 reference scripts, beat by beat

## 1. levitate (146 w / 32.4 s, twist type 2 + 3)
- RULE: Ever since Leo was seven, anyone who lied to him started floating.
- SCALE: Small lie, they'd hover a few inches. Big lie, they'd hit the ceiling. [parallel pair]
- GAGS: A kid told him he'd already done his homework and drifted a foot off the ground. / His sister said she didn't eat the last cookie and had to be pulled down from the light fixture. [escalation: foot -> light fixture]
- HERO: Leo stopped hanging around people. It was easier. [understatement]
- TURN: Then one day a bully shoved him into the lockers and said, I didn't touch you.
- COMP: He went straight up and stopped just under the ceiling tiles. Everyone screamed. The teacher fainted.
- TWIST: Leo looked up and said, That's the highest anyone's ever got. You should be proud. / The bully yelled, I'm not scared of you. He went higher. / He yelled, I don't even care about this. Hi, Leo said, You should stop talking. You're running out of ceiling. [each lie = higher, deadpan hero, one-liner end]

## 2. monster (163 w / 40.2 s, twist type 1)
- RULE: In this world, every school has a monster that patrols the hallways at night, looking for any students still inside.
- SCALE: Ryan's school monster was huge, with long black arms and glowing red eyes, and everyone said that if it ever caught you in school after sunset, you would be dead before morning. (32 words, the longest sentence in all refs: avoid)
- GAGS: So every afternoon, all the students had to quickly leave before it woke up. (only one; this is the horror variant, no jokes)
- TURN: But one day, Ryan fell asleep in class and woke up hours later. The school was completely empty.
- COMP: Then Ryan heard footsteps. The monster was walking down the hallway toward him. Terrified, Ryan hid inside a locker, but the monster kept getting closer. Suddenly it stopped. Ryan looked through the window and saw the monster turn toward the school entrance. The front doors slowly opened. Something tall and pale crawled inside. The school monster charged down the hallway, grabbed the creature, and dragged it screaming back outside.
- TWIST: The monster wasn't hunting the kids. It was protecting them. ["wasn't X. It was Y." reframe]

## 3. whistle (202 w / 45.4 s, twist type 1)
- RULE: In this world, whenever you whistle, you teleport to whoever misses you the most right now.
- SCALE: Whistle at school and you might end up next to your best friend two towns over. Whistle at a party and find out nobody there actually wanted you. [second example is a sting]
- HERO: 15-year-old Tyler tested it constantly.
- GAGS: He whistled during a math test and appeared in his mom's car. She was not happy. / He whistled at a sleepover and landed in his dentist's kitchen. Nobody has explained that one. / ...old fourth grade classroom, standing in front of a substitute who screamed. [understatement buttons]
- TURN: Then one afternoon, he whistled and nothing happened. He tried again. Nothing. He whistled 11 more times in his room like an idiot until his sister told him to stop. [specific number]
- COMP: That night he tried one last time. He vanished instantly. He opened his eyes in a living room he didn't recognize. An old man was sitting alone with the TV off, holding a photo of a kid on a bike.
- TWIST: It was Tyler. The man had been his neighbor until Tyler was six. He taught him how to ride that bike. Nobody had visited him in four years. [sad reframe, flat last line]

## 4. cantcry (212 w / 47.9 s, twist type 1)
- RULE: In this world, nobody is allowed to cry. One tear and you're gone.
- GAGS: So onions are illegal here. Funerals happen over the phone. The movie theater only shows comedies and there's a man at the door who checks. Kids get trained starting at four. Breathe slow, look up at the ceiling, think about something stupid. [5 literal consequences, third has the extra button]
- HERO: Dion hadn't cried since he was four. In fifth grade, a kid hit him in the shin with a bike and he said, ow! and kept walking to class. When his dog died, he hummed for six straight hours.
- TURN: Then one Tuesday, he found out his best friend was moving across the country that afternoon and the car was already in the parking lot.
- COMP: It hit him in the middle of a packed hallway. He did everything they taught him, looked up, held his breath, thought about something stupid. [callback] He lost. One tear went down his face in front of 40 kids and the whole hallway screamed. Then they grabbed him, all of them. Some kid he'd never spoken to had him by the shoulder and nothing happened.
- TWIST: That's when they finally figured it out. Every person who ever got taken was alone when it happened. The rule was never don't cry. It was don't cry alone.

## 5. love (210 w / 49.0 s, twist type 3)
- RULE: In this world, every time you tell someone you love them, you get one extra day of life.
- GAGS: It's not a secret. Everyone knows. It's on the news. Some people are 400 years old. Some people are extremely quiet. [bureaucratic normality, flip pair]
- HERO: Joey's family had always been on the quiet side. His dad said it maybe twice a year, usually by accident. His mom said it at bedtime, which put her comfortably in the 80s.
- TURN: Then one night, Joey found his grandma's birth certificate in a drawer. He did the math four times because he thought he was doing it wrong. She was 1,200 years old.
- COMP: Dialogue ping-pong: Grandma, how old are you? / don't worry about it. / you're older than most countries. / I'm affectionate. / you're immortal. / those are the same thing. Then: She's outlived four husbands, nine presidents, and an entire town that no longer exists. [rule of three, third breaks]
- TWIST: Joey asked if she was ever going to stop. She said, why would I stop? Then she looked at him and said it again. He gained a day too. [rule applied once more, warm button]

## 7. shirtcolor (218 w / 51.4 s, twist type 2 + 3)

- GAGS: You've worn that hoodie for four years. In summer, to a pool. / You're a light gray. You don't get an opinion on anything.

- TWIST: Marcus stood up, straightened the plaid, and the entire parking lot went silent. Every light in the city flickered. Nobody has seen that enforcer since. They just found his shoes.

## 9. jumpscared (481 w / 113.3 s, LONG, first person, twist type 2)
- RULE: In this town, getting scared makes you jump really high. ... boing ... the more scared you are, the higher you go.
- SCALE: A little scare is 10 feet. A big scare is 100.
- GAGS: movie theaters have no roofs, every tree has somebody's shoes, Halloween busiest night for the fire department, mostly ladders, mailman delivered from the church steeple for a week.
- HERO: I'm the jumpiest kid in town. A popped balloon, 40 feet. A sneeze, 60. My own reflection? I landed on the water tower. My mom keeps a giant net in the trunk.
- SECOND CHARACTER + TURN: Ms. Larkin has never jumped, not scared of anything, except heights. Balloon rope snaps with her inside.
- COMP: Boing, she jumps out and back in, firetruck ladder only halfway, everyone looks at me, "I wasn't scared. I was worried. And worried doesn't make you jump." Boo! 20 feet. Rubber spider 30.
- TWIST: "Mom, say my whole name." Benjamin, Alexander, Perez, boing! Rescue, cord, landing. Callback ending: whenever the town needs something off a really high shelf, they don't call the fire department. They call my mom.

## Patterns across all 9
- 6 of 9 open with "In this world / town / a parallel universe"; 1 "Ever since"; 1 "If you're ...".
- Every TURN starts with "Then one ..." / "But one day" / "Last Saturday" / "At the spring fair".
- Every last line is 2-7 words and is either a reframe ("It was X") or a deadpan literal fact.
- Dialogue is always short, said-tags only ("he said", "she said"), often "Hi, Leo said" style inversions.
